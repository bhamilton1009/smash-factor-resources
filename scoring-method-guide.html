The Scoring Method: 10 Rules to Break Your Next Milestone

Golf swing gets all the attention on the range — but score is won or lost by decision-making, tension management, and knowing where you're allowed to be aggressive. This is the on-course framework that's helped players drop 30+ strokes without a swing change.

Core idea: Swing to score, not score to swing
Method: Scoring zone + checkmark system
Applies to: Every handicap level

01 — The Core Shift

Swing to Score, Not Score to Swing

Most instruction is swing-technical. This method flips the question: not "can I get on the green in regulation," but "can I get into position to score, then get down in three or less from there."

The scoring zone isn't the green — it's inside 100 yards. Tour players hit greens in regulation roughly 71% of the time, and they're doing it with a 9-iron or less into most greens. An amateur facing a 200-yard approach with a hybrid isn't "supposed to" hit that green — the target is simply getting into the scoring zone in regulation, then getting down in three.

As a player improves, the "get down in three" target tightens to "get down in two," and the scoring-zone distance shrinks — the system scales with skill instead of demanding perfection early.

02 — Club Selection Philosophy

The Gears of Golf

Not every tee shot calls for driver. Think of club selection like gears in a car — most amateurs live in fourth gear on holes that call for first or second.

1st GearLong iron / hybrid — put it in play, zero risk, just advance the ball.
2nd Gear5-wood — more distance, still controlled.
3rd Gear3-wood — near-full commitment with a safety margin.
4th GearDriver — full send, reserved for holes with width and low consequence for a miss.
Coach's note

"I still feel like I'm in the parking lot. I don't need to be going in fourth gear at 85 mph crashing into cars."

Rule of thumb for gear selection: on stroke-index-one type holes (the hardest hole on the course), or any hole with trouble on both sides and no reward for the aggressive line, drop down a gear. Save 4th gear for the reachable par 5s and the wide-open tee shots — the holes stroke index 13–17, where a mistake doesn't blow up the round.

03 — Scoring System

The Checkmark System

Replace the number on the scorecard with two simple pass/fail questions per hole. This removes the anxiety of watching a running score climb and keeps focus purely on process.

  • Question 1 — Did you get into the scoring zone in regulation?For a par 4, that means inside 100 yards in 2 shots or fewer. Check or no check — nothing else matters yet.
  • Question 2 — Did you get down in 3 shots or less from there?Chip/pitch + 2 putts, or 3 putts from just off the green — either way, check.

Two checks on every hole of a par-4 course produces bogey golf — and bogey golf on every hole is well under 80. The rounds that blow up aren't lost to bogeys; they're lost to doubles and triples. Removing those is the single highest-leverage thing a player can do, at any handicap.

04 — The Framework

10 Rules to Break Your Next Milestone

Use these as an on-course checklist, in order — the early rules set the strategy, the later rules govern the mindset needed to execute it under pressure.

01

Don't Start Aggressive

Rome wasn't built in one day. Stack small wins, build momentum.

The first few holes are where rounds get destroyed. Play conservatively early, especially before the swing and body are warmed up, and save aggressive play for later in the round once confidence and rhythm are established.

02

Don't Be Like Everyone Else

The best path is rarely the most popular one.

Club selection should match what a player can currently execute — not what "everyone hits" off a given tee. Playing a 5-iron off the tee to stay in play is often the smarter decision than reaching for driver just because the hole "calls for it."

03

Take Away the Score

Be addicted to the process, not the outcome.

Stop tracking the running number. Focus only on the two checkmark questions per hole — position to score, then down in three. Removing the score removes the anxiety that builds hole over hole.

04

Be Present

Cherish every moment — you never know what's coming.

Worrying about a future score doesn't help the shot in front of you. Stay with the shot that's actually being played, not the total at the end of the round.

05

Lag Putting Is Everything

You're nothing without speed control.

Great lag putting covers up nearly every other mistake in the round — a blocked chip, a chunked pitch, a mis-clubbed approach. Speed control from 30–50 feet is one of the highest-leverage skills to train.

06

Do Hard Stuff — Start Before You're Ready

You never feel fully ready. Start anyway.

Don't wait for the game to be "together" before playing tournaments or pressure rounds. Get into competitive reps immediately — that's how tension tolerance and course-management instincts actually get built.

07

Train Hard, Play Easy

As Gary Player said: the more I practice, the luckier I get.

Make practice harder than the golf course — pressure putts, tough lies, worst-ball games. Then on the course, take the easy, conservative option every time. When training is harder than playing, the course starts to feel simple.

08

Don't Be a Hero

You can't win a hole with one shot, but you can definitely lose it.

The heroic recovery shot is rarely worth the risk. Take the shot that keeps a double or triple off the card, even if it means giving up on the miracle par.

09

Fully Commit

A 10-out-of-10 commitment, every time.

Once the shot and target are picked, there's no more decision-making — just trust and execution. A shot that's fully committed to and mis-hit is data. A shot that's second-guessed mid-swing is neither a fair test of technique nor of decision-making.

10

Practice Makes Permanence

Repetition builds identity. You fall to the level of your habits.

Practice under the same pressure and routine used on the course — not stress-free reps. What's rehearsed under artificial pressure on the practice green is what shows up on the 18th hole.

05 — On-Course Training

Two Games to Play Once a Week

These two formats build the two skills the scoring method depends on most: discipline under a harder-than-normal constraint, and the ability to go low when conditions are in your favor.

Game 01

Worst-Ball Golf

Hit two balls on every shot, and always play the worse of the two. Forces full commitment on every single swing, since a mis-hit means playing from the worse position — no bailing out on a bad decision.

Builds: discipline, commitment, conservative shot selection.

Game 02

Play It Forward

Play from the forward tees (or reds) and try to shoot as low as possible — driving greens on short par 4s, hitting par 5s in two, attacking flags with wedges.

Builds: comfort making birdies, confidence attacking targets, aggressive-mode reps.

06 — Homework From the Round

The Purposeful Practice Card

Instead of guessing what to work on, let the round tell you. Every miss becomes a specific, counted repetition — not a vague "work on your swing" note.

Build the Card After Every Round

Post-Round Routine
  1. Count every 3-putt from the round. That's the number of 3-putt drill reps owed before the next round.
  2. Count every missed putt inside 4 feet. That's the number of reps for the 4-foot test.
  3. Count every hole not in the scoring zone in regulation. That becomes the number of reps of the "go-to club" drill (the reliable, repeatable tee shot).
  4. Count every missed up-and-down. That becomes the number of chip-and-putt reps owed.
Why it works — the training plan is built directly from what actually happened on the course, not from a generic range session. Over time, most of the "work" shifts away from full-swing technique and toward putting, chipping, and decision-making — because that's usually where the strokes are actually being lost.

The 3-Foot Ladder Drill

Pressure Putting
  1. Place five tees in a circle around the hole, each at 3 feet.
  2. Putt from all five in sequence, going around the circle — the goal is 10 makes in a row (two full laps) before moving on.
  3. Use the exact pre-shot routine that gets used on the course — same pace, same read process, same commitment.
  4. Once 10 in a row is consistent at 3 feet, move the tees back to 3.5 feet, then 4, then 4.5, then 5 — always requiring 10 in a row before advancing.
  5. Set a target score for the next session (e.g., today made 8 of 10 — next session's target is 9).
The feel — this should feel uncomfortable, not easy. A putt that "means something" every time it's hit is what builds the emotional muscle needed to make a 3-footer on the 18th hole with something on the line.
Coach's note

"If I told you for the next 10 days you made 10 in a row from 3 feet, would you question your putting on the course? Your competency level's risen, and so your confidence rises."
