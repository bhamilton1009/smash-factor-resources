Wide & Online: Backswing Sequencing & the Stretch Cycle

A field guide to fixing an inside-and-shut backswing — built from a real coaching session breaking down path, face, and the arm structure that gets you back to the ball on line.

Focus: Backswing width & sequencing
Applies to: Irons & driver
Core fault: Early rotation → inside & shut

01 — Read the Ball

What Your Ball Flight Is Telling You

Before you touch a swing thought, let the ball flight diagnose the swing. Path and face relationship — not feel — decides what you work on next.

Ball FlightPathFaceWhat It Means
Push Too far inside-out Square to the path The path is leaking right, but the face is matching it — starts right and holds.

02 — The "Why"

Two Root Causes of an Inside, Shut Club

Almost every case of push/hook/pull traces back to one (or both) of these two moves happening in the first two feet of the takeaway.

Fault 01

Early Rotation

Hips and shoulders start turning together right out of address instead of the arms and hands creating width first. Everything turns at once — there's no separation, so the club gets dragged around the body instead of staying in front of it.

Fault 02

Flipping the Club Inside

The hands roll the club inside and shut early in the takeaway. Once the face is shut and the path is inside, it doesn't matter what the arms do from there — the club is already stuck on a plane that has to thrust back down too far inside.

Coach's note

"So too much rotation, club slipping inside and shut... From there, if you just keep turning, it doesn't matter about your arm plane — the club's always going to be shut and across."

03 — Foundation

Setup: Get It Right Before You Move

Every fix in this guide is built on top of a correct setup. Rebuild it the same way, every time, in this order.

Step 1 Grip → feet together → club flat on the ground → set posture (hips back, balanced — not on toes or heels).
Step 2 Left foot moves ~4 inches forward from center. This is the ball position for every club except driver.
Step 3 Right foot sets the stance width based on club: wedge = narrow/middle, mid-irons = a touch wider, driver = ball off the left heel, stance widest.

Key handle height check: if the handle is too low or too high at address, chest position changes with it — get handle height correct first, everything else stacks on top of it.

04 — The Engine

The Stretch Cycle: Correct Downswing Sequence

A true release isn't manipulated with the hands — it's the byproduct of the body sequencing correctly. Each link accelerates, then decelerates, handing energy to the next link in order.

Pressure moves into the lead foot first. That creates a ground-reaction force that pushes the body up and rotates it — which is what finally releases the club, not a hand or wrist action timed on purpose.

Coach's note

"I want the left hand to pull the left arm, to pull the left shoulder, to pull the left lat, hip, thigh, knee — in that order. You're stretching, stretching, stretching, and then in transition, push hard — which stretches everything even more."

On release style: a stronger grip with left-wrist flexion (a closed face) generally needs more body rotation to hold the face off (a "body release"). A weaker grip needs the hands and arms to actively release the club through impact to square the face (a "hand/arm release"). Neither is more correct — match the release style to the grip and wrist structure, don't fight it.

05 — Practice

Three Drills for Width & Arm Structure

Use these away from full-speed swings first. Build the position, then add speed once the shape is repeatable.

Wide-and-Stop Backswing

Backswing Width
  1. Set posture. Keep the lower body quiet on the first move — no early hip or shoulder turn.
  2. Rock the shoulders and push the hands/club away, keeping the club outside the hands (not pulled in).
  3. Stop at P2 (club shaft parallel to the ground) and check: is the club a touch outside the hands, feeling open?
  4. From there, slowly turn to the top — let the width of the arms wind up the body rather than the body dragging the arms.
  5. From the top, simply chip the ball out — no need to swing hard. This is a position drill, not a power drill.
The feel — the width of your hands should feel like it's pulling you into your right side, almost stretching you, rather than your body turning and dragging the arms along behind it.

Cross-Handed Arm Structure Drill

Online at the Top
  1. Take your grip, then switch your hands — left hand goes below the right (for a right-handed golfer).
  2. Rock the shoulders away from the ball. The trail (now top) arm will automatically fold on its own.
  3. Keep the stretch on the lead arm as it moves wide and across — this is the feeling of an "online," in-front-of-you arm structure instead of one that gets deep and across.
  4. Switch grip back to normal and repeat the same feeling at speed.
Why it works — it's physically difficult to get the club moving across and behind the body in this cross-handed position, so it teaches the correct arm structure almost automatically.

Club-on-Shoulder Top-of-Backswing Drill

Top Position
  1. Take your grip, then rest the club across the front of your shoulders.
  2. Set your posture, then make a big turn while pushing your arms away from your body.
  3. Note where your arms sit in front of you — wide and away, not wrapped around the body. That's the top-of-backswing position to match.
  4. Return to your normal grip and try to recreate that exact same width and arm position at the top.
The feel — everything stays short and wide in front of you at the top, rather than wrapped around behind your body.
06 — Self-Diagnosis

Error Detection System

Work through this in order, every time you're fixing a bad shot. Don't skip to the downswing until the backswing checks out.

  • Is the club wide on the first move?Club stays outside the hands, not pulled in immediately.
  • Is the club "online" at the top — not across the line?Check the shaft against a plane line, not just how it feels.
  • Is the face square at the top, given your grip?Face position should match your grip and wrist structure — not fight it.
  • Only after 1–3 check out: adjust the downswing shape.If the backswing is correct and the ball still curves, then — and only then — work on path/face manipulation in the downswing (e.g., "feel more of a cut").
Coach's note

"I want you making changes based off what the ball's doing, not based off how you feel... Check the back swing position before you start working into the downswing."

07 — On-Course

5-Step Pre-Shot Routine

Golf is unique — you get to rehearse the shot before you hit it. Use that. The goal is to fill your mind with what you want to happen, not what you're trying to avoid.

1

Decide

Pick the exact shot — yardage, shape, and target line — before anything else.

2

Visualize

Stand behind the ball, lock your eyes on the target, and breathe: in through the nose for 4 seconds, hold for 1, out through the mouth for 4 — all while staring at the target.

3

Feel

Translate the number into a feeling, not a mechanical thought. ("105 yards feels like a 10 o'clock swing.") Avoid stacking technical swing thoughts here.

4

Commit

Step in and execute with zero new thoughts — you're repeating what you already rehearsed in steps 1–3, not solving anything new in the moment.

5

Post-Shot Routine

Every shot goes in one of two buckets: gain confidence from a good one (imprint how it felt), or learn something from a bad one (growth mindset — what will you do differently next time).
